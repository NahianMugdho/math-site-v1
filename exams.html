What each exam tests (quick snapshot)

Exam Maths part (as reported publicly) What to stress
IBA (DU) admission Math section commonly reported as about 30 questions / 30 marks / 30 minutes; pattern changes by year, so check the current circular Arithmetic-heavy: work and pipes, ratio and mixture, percent, profit and loss, compound interest, averages, number sense, speed
GRE Quantitative Reasoning Two scored sections (currently 12 and 15 questions); four content areas: Arithmetic, Algebra, Geometry, Data Analysis; question types: Quantitative Comparison, Multiple Choice (one or more answers), Numeric Entry, Data Interpretation sets High-school maths up to roughly second-year algebra; logic and number sense over formula memorising; no guessing penalty
BCS Preliminary 50th BCS preliminary: Mathematical Reasoning 20 marks and Mental Ability 15 marks out of 200 Fractions, percent, ratio, HCF/LCM, simple geometry, series, coding-decoding, direction and relation problems
Bank recruitment (e.g. Bangladesh Bank AD, Sonali/Janata/Agrani etc.) Preliminary MCQ with an SSC-level maths part (reported as around 30 marks for BB AD) Interest, profit-loss, time and work, average, ratio, basic algebra and geometry; speed matters

Tip for all four: first learn the concept pages above, then drill timed sets. Aim for about one minute per IBA/bank question and under two minutes per GRE question.


Where to Get Full Past Papers and Mocks

  • IBA (DU): the IBA website and its published sample/model papers; coaching-site archives for old admission-test papers.

  • GRE: the free ETS "Official GRE Quantitative Reasoning Practice Questions" book and the ETS Math Review PDF at ets.org/gre.

  • BCS: the Bangladesh Public Service Commission (bpsc.gov.bd) for circulars and the syllabus; past BCS preliminary papers are widely collected in question banks.

  • Bank jobs: the recruiting bank's own job circular (for Bangladesh Bank: erecruitment.bb.org.bd) for the exact exam pattern and marks, plus past-paper question banks.

Because exam patterns and mark distributions change from year to year, always confirm the numbers in the latest official circular before you plan your timing.


How to Use This Part

  1. Learn first, then drill. Read the concept pages in the first part, then do one group a day with a timer.

  2. Mark and review. Circle any problem that took more than twice the target time and redo it two days later.

  3. Mix by exam. For IBA and Bank, spend most time on Groups 4 to 11 (arithmetic). For GRE, add Groups 3, 12, 13, 14 and 15 and practise the quantitative comparison format from the first part. For BCS, cover Groups 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 14, 15 and 16.

  4. Check against official sources. Confirm each exam's current syllabus and mark distribution in the latest official circular before you plan your schedule.
