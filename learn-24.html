২৪. Chapter 3: Circles

  1. Circle M: the set of points at a fixed distance from the centre M.

  2. Circumference (C): the length of the circular line of a circle, i.e. its perimeter.

  3. Radius: a line joining the centre to any point on the circumference (plural: radii).

  4. Diameter: a line through the centre joining two points on the circumference. Its length equals two radii.

  5. π (pi): a constant — the ratio of any circle's circumference to its diameter. It is an irrational number, approximately 3.14 or 22/7.

  6. If any one of r, d, c, A is known, the other three can be found. For radius r: d = 2r, c = 2πr = πd, A = πr² = πd²/4.

    • Example: r = 2 m → d = 4 m, c = 4π m, A = 4π m².

  7. Chord: a line joining any two points on the circumference.

    • a. The diameter is the longest chord.

    • b. The line joining the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

    • c. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

    • d. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

    • e. Chords equidistant from the centre are equal.

    • f. A longer chord is closer to the centre.

    • g. A chord closer to the centre is longer.

  8. Arc: a part of the circumference.

  9. Central and inscribed angles: an arc has a central angle at the centre and inscribed angles on the circumference. If x is the central angle:

    • a. a = b = c, i.e. all inscribed angles on the same arc AC are equal.

    • b. a = b = c = x/2, i.e. an inscribed angle is half the central angle.

    • c. Equal chords cut off equal arcs.

Area of a pie (sector): the area of a pie is found from its central angle. If ∠AC = x°, area of pie = A × x/360° = πr² × x/360°.

  • Example: r = 3 m and AC = 60°. Shaded area = π × 9 × 60/360 = (3/2)π m².

  • A segment and a pie are both parts of a circle's surface.

Tangent: an external line that touches the circle's circumference at exactly one point. The line joining the centre to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.

Angle between the hands (clock problem)

  • A clock's centre has a total angle of 360°. The hour hand moves 30° per hour.

  • The minute hand moves 360° in 60 minutes, i.e. 6° per minute.

  • The hour hand moves ½° per minute.

Some important formulae (from the same page)

  • Length of the altitude drawn from the right-angle vertex of a right triangle to the hypotenuse = (base × perpendicular) / hypotenuse.

  • Equilateral triangle of side a: area = (√3/4) a².

  • If the hypotenuse of a right triangle is twice its base, the perpendicular is √3 times the base.

  • Regular hexagon of side a: area = (3√3/2) a².

  • The two diagonals of a square divide it into four equal isosceles right triangles.

  • The diagonal of a square = √2 × side.

  • Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n − 2)180° / n.

  • If the area of the circle inscribed in an equilateral triangle is πa², the triangle's area = 3√3 a².

  • In an isosceles triangle with an obtuse angle of 120°, the side opposite the 120° angle is √3 times each equal side.

  • In a right triangle with other angles 30° and 60°, the side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse, and the side opposite 60° is √3 times the side opposite 30°.

  • The bisector of an interior angle divides the opposite side internally in the ratio of the two adjacent sides; the bisector of an exterior angle divides it externally in that ratio.
